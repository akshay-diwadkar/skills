# Ideas: establish a weekly writing habit

## 1. Handoff
- State: experiment-first
- Goal: establish a weekly writing habit
- Success measure: one published post per week for 8 weeks
- Baseline / status quo: writes occasionally, no fixed schedule
- Scope: personal practice; no collaborators
- Non-goals: monetization
- Assumptions: two quiet evening blocks per week are available
- Material unknowns: which trigger reliably survives busy weeks
- Decision horizon: 8 weeks
- Decision criteria: consistency, friction, personal fit
- Selected source playbooks: hobby/craft/lifestyle
- Research coverage: two habit-formation overviews
- Research limitations: no external sources beyond two overviews
- Research stop condition: stop after 2 sources or 45 minutes
- Research stop reason: diminishing returns — sources repeated standard habit advice

## 3. Candidate ideas

### I1. Fixed morning writing block
- Mechanism: attach writing to an existing morning cue
- Mechanism category: cue-anchoring
- Why it applies: C1 shows mornings are reliably quiet
- Evidence: E1, C1
- Support basis: evidence-backed: C1, E1
- Decision-criteria fit: high consistency, low friction
- Expected impact: high
- Assumptions and dependencies: morning block stays protected
- Effort: low
- Risk: low
- Confidence: moderate
- What would disconfirm it: missed mornings in the first two weeks
- Cheapest decisive experiment: 2-week trial; metric: sessions completed; pass/fail: 10 of 14; duration: 2 weeks; cost/effort: low

### I2. Public commitment schedule
- Mechanism: announce a weekly post date publicly
- Mechanism category: public-commitment
- Why it applies: C2 shows private plans faded early
- Evidence: C2
- Support basis: evidence-backed: C2
- Decision-criteria fit: good consistency, higher friction
- Expected impact: medium
- Assumptions and dependencies: audience exists to hold the commitment
- Effort: medium
- Risk: medium
- Confidence: low
- What would disconfirm it: skipped weeks despite the announcement
- Cheapest decisive experiment: 4-week schedule; metric: posts published; pass/fail: 3 of 4; duration: 4 weeks; cost/effort: medium

### I3. Two-block flex schedule
- Mechanism: keep two interchangeable writing blocks weekly
- Mechanism category: flex-scheduling
- Why it applies: busy weeks break fixed anchors
- Evidence: none yet — needs testing
- Support basis: hypothesis
- Decision-criteria fit: best personal fit, uncertain consistency
- Expected impact: medium
- Assumptions and dependencies: both blocks stay free weekly
- Effort: low
- Risk: low
- Confidence: low
- What would disconfirm it: neither block survives a busy week
- Cheapest decisive experiment: 4-week trial; metric: sessions completed; pass/fail: 10 of 16; duration: 4 weeks; cost/effort: low

## 5. Recommendation
- Provisional lead: I1 — Fixed morning writing block
- Why it leads: highest consistency with the least friction
- Why it beats rank 2: no external dependency on audience pressure
- Cheapest decisive experiment: 2-week trial; metric: sessions completed; pass/fail: 10 of 14; duration: 2 weeks; cost/effort: low
- What could change the ranking: evidence that morning blocks fail in busy weeks
- Conditions that would change the ranking: I1 misses 5+ sessions while I3 keeps its pace
- How decision criteria were applied: consistency was primary, then friction; personal fit demoted I2 despite its promise

## 6. Contradictions and open questions
- Strongest challenge to rank 1: morning anchor may break on travel weeks
- Baseline / status quo comparison: any scheduled option beats the current sporadic pattern
- Condition for a different winner: I2 wins if accountability turns out necessary
- Remaining contradiction or uncertainty: C1 covers one month; whether it generalizes across seasons is untested
