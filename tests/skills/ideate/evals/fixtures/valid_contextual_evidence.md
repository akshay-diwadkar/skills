# Ideas: choose a first instrument for a child

## 1. Handoff
- State: decision-ready
- Goal: choose a first instrument for a child
- Success measure: child practices 3+ times weekly for 3 months
- Baseline / status quo: no instrument yet
- Scope: age-appropriate acoustic options
- Non-goals: professional training path
- Assumptions: home practice space is available
- Material unknowns: the child's long-term preference
- Decision horizon: before next term starts
- Decision criteria: engagement, cost, noise, lesson availability
- Selected source playbooks: hobby/craft/lifestyle
- Research coverage: teacher conversations, local availability
- Research limitations: no formal studies consulted
- Research stop condition: stop after 3 sources or 1 hour
- Research stop reason: condition met

| C2 | A neighbor lends a practice keyboard | user-provided | confirmed this week |

### External evidence

## 3. Candidate ideas

### I1. Keyboard first
- Mechanism: start with the borrowed keyboard
- Mechanism category: keyboard-start
- Why it applies: C2 removes the main cost barrier
- Evidence: C2, C3
- Support basis: evidence-backed: C2
- Decision-criteria fit: strong on cost, good on engagement
- Expected impact: high
- Assumptions and dependencies: the loan lasts the term
- Effort: low
- Risk: low
- Confidence: moderate
- What would disconfirm it: no interest after 3 weeks
- Cheapest decisive experiment: term trial; metric: weekly practice sessions; pass/fail: 3 of 7; duration: 3 months; cost/effort: free

### I2. Voice lessons
- Mechanism: weekly singing class instead of an instrument
- Mechanism category: voice-first
- Why it applies: C1 shows daily musical engagement
- Evidence: C1, C3
- Support basis: evidence-backed: C1, C3
- Decision-criteria fit: best on engagement, weak on lessons availability
- Expected impact: medium
- Assumptions and dependencies: a fitting class exists
- Effort: medium
- Risk: medium
- Confidence: low
- What would disconfirm it: class fills before enrolment
- Cheapest decisive experiment: enrol one term; metric: sessions attended; pass/fail: 8 of 10; duration: 1 term; cost/effort: medium

### I3. Ukulele at home
- Mechanism: buy a low-cost ukulele for self-practice
- Mechanism category: ukulele-start
- Why it applies: low noise suits home practice
- Evidence: none beyond general knowledge
- Support basis: assumption-backed: a quiet home instrument holds engagement
- Decision-criteria fit: good on noise and cost, weak on lessons
- Expected impact: medium
- Assumptions and dependencies: the child tolerates self-directed practice
- Effort: low
- Risk: low
- Confidence: low
- What would disconfirm it: self-practice stalls without a teacher
- Cheapest decisive experiment: month trial; metric: weekly practice sessions; pass/fail: 3 of 4; duration: 1 month; cost/effort: low

## 5. Recommendation
- Provisional lead: I1 — Keyboard first
- Why it leads: free instrument with an existing slot history
- Why it beats rank 2: borrows the instrument, no purchase decision needed
- Cheapest decisive experiment: term trial; metric: weekly practice sessions; pass/fail: 3 of 7; duration: 3 months; cost/effort: free
- What could change the ranking: the child strongly resisting keyboard practice
- Conditions that would change the ranking: I2 gains a guaranteed slot, or I1 practice drops below twice weekly
- How decision criteria were applied: cost and lesson availability led; engagement confirmed the order rather than changing it

## 6. Contradictions and open questions
- Strongest challenge to rank 1: borrowing delays the decision if the keyboard is reclaimed
- Baseline / status quo comparison: all options beat doing nothing, and I1 is the only zero-cost start
- Condition for a different winner: I2 wins if a mid-term slot opens
- Remaining contradiction or uncertainty: none remaining — C2 and C3 are current, and C1 supports rather than contradicts
